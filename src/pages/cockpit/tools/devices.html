Get oshal on your devices

Your swarm lives at this address. Put it on the screens you actually use: a desktop that does work for you, a phone that carries the cockpit, a TV that shows and speaks Jarvis. Everything on this page points back at that same swarm, and none of it needs an operator.

Desktop

Make a Windows PC a worker node. The swarm can then run a browser on it, execute shell tasks and drive the screen, and only work dispatched to you ever reaches it.

Saved as install-oshal-node.cmd. Copy it to the PC and double-click it.
  1. Node.js 20 or newer must be installed on that PC first (nodejs.org, the LTS build).
  2. Windows may ask whether to run a file from the internet. Choose Run.
  3. It installs the node app, binds it to your account and starts it. The computer appears below within a minute.
  4. Delete the file afterwards. It holds a credential for that one computer.

Windows only today. macOS and Linux are not one-click yet; the guide says what exists for them.

Phone

The cockpit installs as an app on iPhone and Android. Scan, sign in, add it to your Home Screen.

  1. iPhone: open it in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  2. Android: open it in Chrome, tap the three-dot menu, then Install app (older builds say Add to Home screen).

Want the phone as the microphone for a TV? Open the Jarvis remote on it: push to talk, and the TV shows and speaks the answer.

TV

OSHAL Home turns a Fire TV, Roku or Samsung TV into a Jarvis screen. It shows and speaks the answers, lists your tasks and live bots, and your phone is the microphone.

The TV apps are not in the app stores yet. Each one installs in the TV's developer mode from a build, and the guide walks through it for each platform.

How to install on a TV Approve a TV code

Already running the TV app? It shows a code on screen. Approve a TV code signs that TV in to your account.

The desktop installer carries a credential bound to one computer and to you. It can be revoked at any time, which disables that one computer and nothing else. The phone and TV codes carry no secret at all; they only open pages on this swarm that still ask you to sign in.
