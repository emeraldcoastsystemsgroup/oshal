And what the lab found that's still alive

The keyword scorer died — but the audit that killed it found something better. An FDA-approval headline landed 66 minutes before an +11.9% move, and every count-and-volume metric in the system missed it. One headline, read properly, was worth more than every statistic we were tracking.

That is the open hypothesis — a language model reading the news, rather than counting it — and it is exactly the kind of question this box exists to answer: your subscription writes the reader, the local model backtests it overnight for free, and the lab tells you the truth. 07 / RISK

The two things that could kill it

Risk 1 · a component-price emergency is underway

The 2026 LPDDR5X shortage is repricing this whole category right now. One competitor's 128 GB box went from $1,985 to $4,349 — and our supplier's own site carries a "price increase coming soon" banner. Launching a hardware business into a memory squeeze is genuinely dangerous.

Mitigation: build-to-order only (never hold stock), 30-day quotes, and lead with the $299 tier — which moves component-price risk to the customer entirely.

Risk 2 · the legal line is thin, and it shapes the product

CFTC v. Vartuli (2d Cir. 2000): a vendor sold software that emitted buy/sell signals customers followed mechanically. That made him an unregistered trading advisor — and mechanical signal output got no First Amendment protection.

So the product is designed around it: we ship an engine, never a strategy service. No signals ever leave us — we have no channel through which a trade instruction could travel. No discretion, no personalisation, no cut of trades, no AUM fee. Composer chose to register as an RIA because it supplies model portfolios and holds discretion. QuantConnect and 3Commas did not, because they don't. We must stay firmly on the QuantConnect side of that line — and a securities lawyer signs off before a dollar changes hands.

The form factor is the compliance story

Schwab's individual developer keys are "unique to you… may not provide any third party with access." A vendor-hosted SaaS driving customer accounts needs a commercial tier and approval. An appliance where the buyer registers their own app and the keys never leave their own hardware fits both Alpaca's and Schwab's individual-use models exactly. The box isn't just a privacy story — it is the cleanest regulatory posture available.

09 / VERDICT

The recommendation

Run Phase 1 as a 25-unit, build-to-order experiment, and lead with the $299 Certified Build — no inventory, no warranty, no exposure to the memory crunch. It tests demand, support load, and the legal posture for roughly the cost of one machine.

If the support number holds, scale it. If it doesn't, you learned the truth cheaply and the open-source project is completely unharmed — because the software was always going to be free.

The moat

Every competitor in this category markets an edge. In a market where the SEC is actively fining firms for "AI-washing," the credible player wins by being the one who publishes what didn't work. We already do. That discipline is free, it's already in the repository, and a hype-driven competitor structurally cannot copy it.
